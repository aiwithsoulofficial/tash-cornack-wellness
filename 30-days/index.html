The Sceptic's 30 Days

One video a day. You test one use of the water, you say honestly whether it worked, and you post it either way. You are not the salesperson here. You are the person who does not believe it yet.

Why this works

Four reasons, and they are the whole strategy.

1It needs no expertise. You are testing, not teaching. You never have to be the authority.
2It needs no permission. You are documenting your own house. Nobody has to approve it.
3It cannot be faked. People can see a real kitchen and a real result. That builds trust faster than any claim.
4The failures are the point. When something doesn't work and you say so, you become the only person in this space anyone believes.

Your four pillars

Every video is one of these four. Each day below is already tagged, so you never have to decide what kind of video you're making.

The Sceptic

The test itself. You open doubting it. "I bet this doesn't work." Then you do it on camera, one take, and see.

The Verdict

The honest call. True or false. Worked or didn't. No hedging, no softening the ones that flopped.

The Retest

Something failed. You went back to the people who know the system, asked what you did wrong, and you're running it again.

The Tash

The musings and the wonderings. Your house, your boys, your gut feel. The woo, because that's who you are and people buy from people.

The rules, before you film anything

→One 60-second phone video a day. One take. No editing, no script, no lighting setup. If you re-record it three times it stops being believable.
→Say what you're testing, do it, say what happened. That's the whole format. Every single day.
→Never say a health word. No heal, treat, cure, fix, reduce, prevent, detox. Not once, not as a joke, not in a caption.
→Say "I noticed", never "it does". You are reporting what you observed in your house. You are not making a claim about anybody else.
→Don't name the brand and don't mention the business. It's "my water machine". No company name, no income talk, no opportunity. That keeps you safe and keeps practitioners watching.
→If it doesn't work, post it anyway. Those will be your best videos. Then turn it into a Retest.

End every single video the same way

"The full list of everything I've tested is on my website."

That's it. That one line is what turns thirty videos into a list that belongs to you. Don't sell, don't pitch, don't explain. Just point.

When something doesn't work

This is the bit most people get wrong, and it's where your credibility actually gets built.

The retest loop

1. It failed. Post it. Say plainly that it didn't work for you.

2. Go back to the people who know this system properly and ask what you did wrong. Is there a trick to it? A different pH? A longer soak?

3. Come back on camera: "People who understand this a lot better than me reckon I should have done it this way instead."

4. Run the test again. Post whatever happens, including if it fails twice.

One failure becomes three videos, and you come out of it looking like the most honest person on the internet. That is worth more than any test that works first go.

Before you start

Don't start until the machine is plugged in and you have internet. You move on the 20th. Aim for the week of 25 August and don't lose a minute of sleep over the gap.

→Film day one and day two on the same afternoon so you start with a buffer. The first missed day is what kills these.
→If you miss a day, skip it and keep going. Don't double up and don't start over.
→You are not selling anything for thirty days. You're just the woman testing things in her kitchen.

You've already lived the hard version of this. Thirty days of telling the truth about a water machine is the easy part.
